# Product opportunity — Commercial Director Platform

Status: Draft | Owner: Dan (Product owner) | Last reviewed: 2026-09-19
Applicability: Applies | Evidence baseline: Web research, 19 Sep 2026; Dan's own 20 years commercial-leadership experience
Related requirements / ADRs: None yet | Next review: On thesis validation or pivot

## Problem and user

- **Product thesis:** For an SME owner/leader running £1m–£15m revenue without a dedicated Commercial Director, we turn scattered pipeline and financial data into a single, decisive verdict — are we on target, is the pipeline good enough to get there, and what's working well enough to double down on.
- **Primary user:** SME founder/MD or ops lead who already runs *some* formal pipeline process — a CRM or a tracked deal spreadsheet — but lacks a defined pipeline target and can't connect pipeline behaviour to financial outcome. **Explicitly excludes businesses with no pipeline concept at all** (validated as a distinct, wrong-fit segment — see interview evidence below); that's a different product (pipeline literacy, not pipeline judgment).
- **Secondary user:** none in v1. Non-goal, not a role we design for yet.
- **Job to be done:** When I review the business, I want to know *before* the quarter ends whether we're on target and what in the pipeline needs to change to get there — not just a P&L from my accountant after the fact that tells me what happened but not what to do — so I can redirect effort toward what's working while there's still time to act.
- **Current workflow and pain:** Validated via 1:1 conversations with business owners (count/sample not yet logged — see open questions). Consistent pattern: pipeline tracked in a spreadsheet reviewed monthly by total value only; no defined pipeline target to check against; deals sit unflagged for long periods ("some have been there for ages"); financial position comes from the accountant only after quarter-end and carries no pipeline-improvement guidance. One owner's framing: *pipeline looks full, but we're behind target* — volume without quality, undetected until it's too late to fix.
- **Why now:** AI-assisted financial modelling and reporting pipelines that used to be cost-prohibitive for small businesses are now commercially available — cited as an active trend in fractional-CFO service delivery in 2026. This lowers the cost of building the judgment layer, but the same shift is arming competitors, not just us.

## Opportunity and viability

Reachable users: UK SMEs £1m–£15m revenue without a full-time Commercial Director — a real but unsized segment. **No credible market-sizing has been done; treat any TAM figure as invented until sourced.** Adoption constraint: the buyer (owner/MD) is also usually the one who has to trust a machine-generated verdict enough to act on it commercially — that trust is the actual product risk, not the data pipeline. Operating cost: financial-data integration (accounting APIs) carries meaningfully higher engineering and compliance overhead than CRM integration; deferring it (per the manual-upload validation route above) keeps early operating cost low. **Pricing anchor (Sep 2026):** ~£100–£200/month for a manual-upload version, sourced from Dan's personal network. That's a real number, not a placeholder, and it clears the "beats do-nothing, undercuts HubSpot" bar from doc 01's alternatives table. What it doesn't yet answer is acquisition cost — every respondent already trusted Dan before seeing the product, which is not a repeatable channel. Treat the price as validated; treat the business model as not, until there's a customer who didn't come through Dan's own relationships.

## Success and boundaries

| Outcome | Baseline | Target and time horizon | Measurement | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A pilot SME leader (not Dan) finds the "on target" verdict accurate and actionable | Unknown — no pilot yet | 3+ pilot users say the verdict changed a decision, within 8 weeks of first use | Structured follow-up interview post-pilot | Dan |
| Pipeline-quality + financial synthesis verdict matches what a human CFO/CRO would independently conclude | Unknown | ≥80% directional agreement on a sample of test months | Blind comparison against Dan's own manual judgment, then a third-party fractional CFO/CRO | Dan |

- **Non-goals (v1):** Businesses with no existing pipeline concept (validated distinct segment — pipeline literacy is a different product); live accounting API integration (Xero OAuth) deferred until the judgment engine is validated with manual/CSV-uploaded P&L and pipeline data — downgraded from "high-risk, indefinitely deferred" to "defined, buildable v1.1" now that Xero is confirmed as the dominant target; win-driver attribution and capacity-matched allocation (segment 3) — **decided v2, not v1**, built on the same engine once segment 2's verdict is proven trusted; marketing and procurement modules; multi-user/team roles; anything resembling a full ERP.
- **Key risks:** (1) Desirability — trust is conditional, not given: the verdict must be explainable ("proof, and an understanding of how the tool works it out"), not a black-box score. This is now a hard product requirement, not just a risk to monitor. (2) Feasibility — judgment quality depends on messy, inconsistently-defined CRM stage data (a documented industry-wide problem, not unique to us) *and* on financial data quality once Xero integration is built. (3) Viability — pricing has to beat "do nothing" (the real incumbent, confirmed by interviews) and land clearly below whatever made HubSpot feel too expensive to these buyers; no actual price point tested yet. (4) Competitive — ScaleWithCFO-style products are already closer to full-combo than a from-scratch build; a slow build gets flanked.
- **Cheapest validation:** 5–10 structured interviews with SME leaders outside Glowcroft, followed by a manual-data pilot (CSV/upload, no live integration) with 1–2 real businesses. Pass/fail: if pilot users don't say the verdict changed a real decision, the thesis is wrong as scoped — stop before building integrations.
- **Stop/pivot conditions:** Pilot users say they don't trust a machine-only verdict without a human attached (pivots this toward a human-in-the-loop or advisory-augmentation product, not pure SaaS). Or: pilot users only want the financial side, not pipeline quality (pivots toward direct ScaleWithCFO-style competition, a much harder fight).
